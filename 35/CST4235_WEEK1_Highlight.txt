Privacy and Data governance: 
    NYC Taxi dAtaset (2014): Case study
        - 173 Million Records released to support open data researchers
        - Inclusion: Drop off, Pick up points, times, fares and taxi driver iDs 
        - Exclusions: Names, Phone Number
        - Researchers / journalists were able to identify: specific drivers and conduct income estimates, track passengers(when and where they travelled)
        - Findings: Location Time Data can act as : Quasi-Identifiers, hence making re-identification possible
            - Limits of anonymisation are observed, what is there to say for?
            - Why Time Location Data is risky?
                : Due to having time stamps, one could only be at one place at a one specific time
            - What could the City conduct differently to protect privacy while still enabling research?
                : Round up time to mimic the duration spent for data accuracy, but no actual values are displayed
                : Cryptographic hashing of the IDs for taxi drivers not to be identifiable
                : Use of generalised locations rather than exact GPS coordinates
                "The Limits of Anonymization
                        This case demonstrates that traditional anonymization (like data stripping or masking) is insufficient for high-dimensional datasets. Removing direct identifiers like names and phone numbers does not equal true anonymity. When data is rich enough, it can be combined with external, publicly available information (such as celebrity paparazzi photos with visible timestamps, or public check-ins) to reverse-engineer identities. This proves that data can be easily re-identified without ever possessing explicit personal details.
                        Why Time-Location Data is Risky
                        Time-location data is inherently dangerous because human mobility patterns are highly unique and predictable.
                        • Quasi-Identifiers: A single coordinate might mean nothing, but a sequence of timestamped locations (e.g., leaving a specific suburban home at 7:30 AM and arriving at a specific office at 8:15 AM) uniquely fingerprints an individual.
                        • Low Entropy of Human Movement: Research shows that just four spatio-temporal points are enough to uniquely identify 95% of individuals in a mobile dataset.
                        • Sensitive Inferences: Location tracking exposes highly sensitive, private habits, including visits to medical clinics, political rallies, religious venues, or romantic partners, creating immense potential for blackmail, stalking, or corporate surveillance.
                        What the City Could Do Differently
                        To balance public research utility with individual privacy, the city could implement the following data protection frameworks:
                        Strategy	Technical Implementation	How It Protects Privacy
                        Differential Privacy	Mathematical noise is injected into the dataset before release.	Ensures that an observer cannot definitively prove whether a specific individual's data was included in the dataset, masking exact habits.
                        Data Aggregation & K-Anonymity	Grouping individual points into broader categories (e.g., rounding pickup times to the nearest 15–30 minutes and grouping locations into larger postal zones/neighborhoods instead of exact GPS coordinates).	Guarantees that an individual's trip looks identical to at least \(k-1\) other people's trips, preventing single-out tracking.
                        Cryptographic Hashing with Salt	Instead of poorly masked or raw driver IDs, use strong, salted cryptographic hash functions changed frequently.	In the actual NYC case, driver IDs were hashed using MD5 without a salt, allowing researchers to easily crack them via brute-force. Proper salting prevents this.
                        Tiered Access / Synthetic Data	Releasing entirely artificial "synthetic datasets" that mimic real traffic patterns for general public research, while restricting raw data to a secure virtual enclave for vetted researchers.	Keeps raw location data entirely out of the public domain while still providing statistical utility for urban planning."
